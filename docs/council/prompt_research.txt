Research task: benchmark a draft syllabus for a short applied machine-learning fundamentals course against well-regarded existing curricula and against the research evidence on learning, then report gaps and concrete recommendations.

The learner: a working data analyst (strong SQL and BI, basic statistics, reads but does not write Python, almost no ML) whose analysis is now largely produced by an AI coding assistant; the goal is to be able to frame ML problems, choose approaches, brief the assistant and audit its output - not to hand-code models. Prefers audio/video, wants short lessons, learns fast but forgets without reinforcement.

The draft syllabus is attached. 

Please research and report, with sources:
A. How do the best-regarded introductory/applied ML curricula sequence and scope the fundamentals (for example: Google's Machine Learning Crash Course, fast.ai Practical Deep Learning / Practical ML for coders, Andrew Ng's Machine Learning Specialization, "An Introduction to Statistical Learning", Kaggle Learn, Microsoft and AWS ML fundamentals paths, and any course aimed specifically at analysts or managers such as "AI for Everyone" or data-literacy programmes)? Build a comparison of which core topics each includes. Which topics appear in most of them but are missing or thin in the attached draft? Which topics in the draft are unusual for a fundamentals course, and are they justified for this learner?
B. What do practitioners and published guidance identify as the most common, most costly mistakes in applied ML projects (leakage, evaluation errors, misuse of metrics, causal over-claiming, deployment/monitoring failures)? Does the draft cover each? Cite sources such as published checklists (for example "Rules of Machine Learning", the ML Test Score, REFORMS/leakage surveys, "Hidden technical debt") and note anything from the last two years about auditing AI-assistant-generated analysis code.
C. What does the learning-science evidence say about retention for short narrated/audio lessons with quizzes: spaced repetition intervals, retrieval practice, interleaving, pretesting, worked examples, segmenting and signalling in multimedia learning, optimal lesson length, and audio-only learning? Give specific, evidence-backed recommendations for this course's format (5-7 minute narrated lessons with synchronised visuals, a pre-question, five scenario questions, spaced review at 1/3/7/14/30/60 days, case files, labs).
D. A prioritised list of at most 12 concrete changes to the draft, each tied to the evidence above.
Keep the report tight and practical.
