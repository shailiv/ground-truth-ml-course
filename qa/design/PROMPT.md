You are a senior product designer and a demanding art director. Review the attached screenshots of a private learning
website, critically and specifically. The owner's acceptance criterion is, in their words: "the site is pretty,
engaging and not confusing". Your job is to find everything that falls short of that, and to say exactly how to fix it.

## The product
"Ground Truth" - a private course that teaches machine-learning fundamentals to one working data analyst (strong in
SQL and dashboards, little Python, almost no ML, learns by listening and watching, short sessions, laptop and phone).
37 short lessons. Each lesson: Guess (one question) -> Watch (a narrated slide player, about 5 minutes; some slides are
interactive widgets) -> Check (4 questions) -> Explain (optional) -> Take away. Around it: a daily spaced-repetition
Review, Practice (case files to audit, labs to run with an AI assistant), audio Drills, a Field guide, About.

## The intended design language
A surveyor's field book: cool paper and green-black ink, one survey-teal accent, an amber "you are here" marker,
a wide stage that plays like a video while everything around it stays quiet. Display face Bricolage Grotesque, text in
Inter, numbers and code in JetBrains Mono. Light and dark themes. Charts: blue for the default/"stayed" series, orange
for the emphasised/"churned" series, thin marks, hairline grids, labels in ink colours not series colours.

## The screenshots
File names say what each one is (01-06 home pages, 07-19 slides and widgets inside the player, 21-28 other pages,
30-35 phone at 390 px wide, 40-41 sign-in). "first-visit" = a brand new learner; "in-progress" = four lessons done and
review cards due. Desktop shots are 1440 x 900 unless they are full-page captures.

## What I want from you
Be concrete and unsentimental. No praise sandwiches. For every problem: which screenshot, what exactly is wrong
(point to the element), why it hurts (pretty / engaging / not confusing), and the fix as a designer would spec it
(sizes, spacing, weight, colour role, layout change, copy change). Look especially for:
- hierarchy: is the one thing to do next unmistakable on every screen? competing primary buttons?
- the slide stage: empty space, cramped or floating content, type scale, how the eight or so slide types hold together
  as one system, whether widgets look like part of that system, chart quality (labels, legends, axes, colours)
- consistency: buttons, pills, cards, spacing rhythm, corner radii, icon style, capitalisation, tone of microcopy
- first-time comprehension: would a new learner understand what the site is, what a lesson involves, what Review,
  Practice, Drills and Field guide are, without being told?
- engagement: does anything feel flat, generic, like a template, or like homework? what would make it feel alive
  without becoming a game?
- light versus dark: does either theme look like an afterthought? contrast problems?
- phone: touch targets, wasted space, text size, anything cut off or awkward
- anything that looks unfinished, misaligned, or simply ugly

## Output format
1. **Verdict**: scores out of 10 for pretty, engaging, not confusing, with two sentences each.
2. **The ten changes that would improve it most**, ranked, each with screenshot(s), problem, and an exact fix.
3. **Everything else**, grouped by screen, as a list of problem -> fix.
4. **What already works and must not be touched** (short).
5. **One idea** that would give the site a memorable identity, consistent with the field-book language.
