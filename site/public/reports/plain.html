Churn model: what it can and cannot do

Rows: one per advertiser, 12,000 in six monthly snapshots. Trained on Jan-Mar (6,000 advertisers, 11.1% churned). April left out: its 60-day outcomes were not yet known on the day May is scored. Tested once on May-Jun (4,000 advertisers, 527 churned, 13.2%), which the model never saw. Churned = no spend at all in the 60 days after the snapshot. Model: logistic regression, 17 columns from the 8 weeks before the snapshot. Simulated data.

What was found

Of the 200 advertisers the model ranked highest, 136 really churned (95% interval 123 to 150). A random 200 would hold about 26. At the size of the list the team uses today, the model also does better than the hand rule: the rule "spend trend below 0.7" flags 711 advertisers, of whom 246 churned; the model's top 711 hold 321.

Ranking quality: AUC 0.845 on the test months (95% interval 0.827 to 0.863), 0.849 on the training months. The two are close, so the model is not memorising.

What not to conclude

Accuracy says nothing here. The model is 88.5% accurate at a 0.5 cut-off; predicting that everyone stays is 86.8% accurate and finds no one.
The scores are not percentages you can add up. On the test months the model said 10.9% on average and 13.2% churned. Use the scores to rank, not to total revenue at risk.
The list leans towards small accounts. The top 400 hold 55% of the small-account churners and 25% of the large-account ones, while large accounts are 67% of the spend lost to churn.
Nothing here says what causes churn, or that calling a listed advertiser changes anything. That needs a test.
